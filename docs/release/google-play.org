#+TITLE: Kolahal on Google Play
#+STARTUP: showall

Kolahal is in testing on Google Play: the first bundle (0.4.6-dev, versionCode 15, the Play build
with notification access) was approved in internal testing on 26 October 2026, and the Release
workflow has uploaded to the closed test since 0.4.8. It isn't in production yet. This is what is
ready, what Play's policy asked for, and the steps left. Each bundle needs a higher versionCode
than the last.

* Before production

Play needs a closed test before production: 12 or more testers opted in for 14 days in a row,
then Dashboard → Apply for production (review usually takes 7 days or less). Testers who opt out
before day 14 don't count.

* On Play: the Play build, and why it has less

Google Play gets the Play build, the full app without three things, packaged as a bundle:
=./gradlew :app:bundlePlay= (the Release workflow too). What it leaves out:

| Left out                                  | What goes with it                                  | Play's rule                                                                                                                                            |
|-------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =READ_CALL_LOG=                           | calls in the log, =calls=                          | not allowed: Play's SMS and Call Log policy names "calls appearing in wallpaper, launcher, and other tools" as invalid, and launchers get no exception |
| =REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES=                | =update= and its code                              | Device and Network Abuse: an app updates only through Play                                                                                             |
| =READ_CONTACTS=                           | contacts read by name; the picker instead (below)  | Play's Contacts policy (apps targeting Android 17, from 27 January 2027): broad access only where Android's contact picker can't do the job, with a declaration |

The maintainer chose (26 October 2026) to leave the call log and the accessibility service out
rather than risk the declaration. The call log stays out. The accessibility service (=LockService=,
the screen lock) is in both builds since the maintainer's choice of 26 November 2026, with Play's
accessibility declaration, the disclosure before Android's settings open, and a line in the store
listing: see [[*The accessibility declaration][The accessibility declaration]] below.

Display over other apps (=SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW=) is in both builds: the maintainer chose (25 November 2026)
to have it in the Play build too, with the setting =screen.shader-over-apps=, =allow overlay= and the
window that plays a one-off shader over the app in front. Google's review approved 0.5.17, the first
build with it and with the live wallpaper, on the day it was sent (25 November 2026). No rule of Play's forbids it and it has no
declaration form; Play's permission rules ask that the feature be in the store listing (it is, under A
SCENE BEHIND IT), that the access be asked for in context after an explanation, and that the app work
without it. So the setting is off until the user turns it on, on the Shaders tab; before Android's page
opens, =OverlayAccessDialog= says what is drawn, when and for how long, that touches go through and that
nothing of the app in front is read, and only *Allow* opens the page (=allow overlay= does the same).
Without the access a one-off plays over home, as before. The window takes no touch or focus and is no
more opaque than Android passes touches through (=InputManager.getMaximumObscuringOpacityForTouch=), and
Android Go and other low-memory phones, which can't give the access, aren't offered it.

If Google refuses it, the Play build drops it again with two changes and nothing else in the code: in
=app/build.gradle.kts=, the =play= build type's =buildConfigField("boolean", "SHADER_OVER_APPS", "true")=
becomes ="false"=, and =app/src/play/AndroidManifest.xml= gets back
=<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW" tools:node="remove" />=. Every
place that reads the switch then hides the setting, =allow overlay= and the window, and R8 drops the
window's code. Take its sentence out of the store listing too.

How a reviewer sees it, for App content → App access if Play asks: on the Shaders tab, under the
one-off shaders, Play over other apps asks for notification access and Display over other apps; allow
both (Kolahal explains each first), then turn on Play over other apps. Give =hearts= a word in its
details (or run =shader words hearts test=), open another app, and send the phone a notification with
that word: hearts play over the app for a few seconds, and touches go through them.

Neither build has =ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK=. The maintainer removed it (1 November 2026), with
=@uses lan= and =local-network=: from 25 August 2027, Play lets an app targeting Android 17 ask for
it only with a declaration, for core functionality a device picker can't serve. Kolahal doesn't check
where a request goes: on Android 17, Android blocks the local network itself.

Notification access is in (since 0.4.6): notifications in the log, =notifs=, media controls and
=@on notification= scripts. Play has no declaration form for a notification listener; its User
Data policy wants an in-app disclosure with consent before the access, so =allow read-notifications= (from the
prompt, Setup or the tour) shows what Kolahal reads, what for and that it stays on the phone, and
opens Android's Notification access screen only on *Allow* (=NotificationAccessDialog=). The Data
safety answer stays "no data collected": notifications never leave the phone. The Play-build APK on
GitHub has it too, so Play Protect can block it when sideloaded in India and some other countries,
like the full build.

Contacts are out since 0.4.7, by the same choice: no declaration. =call=, =sms= and =contacts= open
Android's contact picker (=ContactPicker=: Android 17's =ACTION_PICK_CONTACTS= session, before it
=ACTION_PICK= on phone numbers), which lets Kolahal read only the person picked. Kolahal keeps that
person's name and numbers on the phone (=picked_contacts= prefs, out of Kolahal's backup file and
of cloud backups) so the name
works by itself next time; =contacts --forget <name>= drops one. The Data safety answer stays "no
data collected": they never leave the phone.

Everything else stays, because Play doesn't restrict it or needs only a clear runtime request:
calendar, exact alarms (=SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM=, the one the user grants; the restricted
one is =USE_EXACT_ALARM=), usage access, system settings, Do Not Disturb, the private space, the
status bar, uninstall requests, setting the wallpaper, and the app list (Kolahal uses =<queries>=, not the restricted
=QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES=). Scripts in Lua and JavaScript are allowed: Play forbids downloading
executable code (dex, JAR, .so), not interpreted code, as long as it can't be used to break Play's
policy.

Since 0.4.4 a script's daily time runs in a short foreground service (=ScriptRunService=, type
=shortService=, =FOREGROUND_SERVICE= only, no =FOREGROUND_SERVICE_*= type permission) for its few
seconds: Android lets a launcher pause or resume the work profile only in front, and the run
reaches the network while the phone dozes. If Play Console asks about it under App content →
Foreground service permissions: it runs a script the user installed, at a time the user set, for
seconds, started by an exact alarm the user allowed.

=tools/check-play-build.sh= checks the Play APK: it fails if any of these appear: the
call log, SMS or contacts permissions, =QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES=, =USE_EXACT_ALARM=, =REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES=,
=MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE=, =ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION=, =ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK=, an accessibility service other than the screen lock's, a screen lock service marked =isAccessibilityTool= or able to retrieve window content, or the self-updater (its address in the code, its file provider or its receiver). It also fails if =SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW= and the window for shaders over other apps (its name in the code) don't come together: the Play build has both, and a build without the feature has neither. Both
workflows run it, so a library that adds one is caught.

* The accessibility declaration

The screen lock is an accessibility service (=gesture/LockService.kt=): Android lets an app lock the
screen only through one (=performGlobalAction(GLOBAL_ACTION_LOCK_SCREEN)=). It is in the Play build
since the maintainer's choice of 26 November 2026. A launcher is not an accessibility tool, so Play
wants three things: the disclosure in the app, a line in the store listing (under BY TOUCH), and the
declaration in Play Console with a video. Play Console asks for the declaration under App content
once a bundle with the service is uploaded; the bundle can't go to review until it is filled in.

What the service is, as built: off until the user turns it on in Android's accessibility settings;
no events (no =accessibilityEventTypes=), =canRetrieveWindowContent= false, no flags, not marked
=isAccessibilityTool=; its code calls =performGlobalAction= once, to lock, and nothing else.
=tools/check-play-build.sh= fails if the Play APK has another accessibility service, or this one can
retrieve window content or is marked as a tool.

The disclosure: =allow lock= (from the prompt, the setup checklist, the tour, or a tap on the line
that =lock=, the Lock screen quick action or a lock gesture leaves while the service is off) first
shows =LockAccessDialog=, "Let Kolahal lock the screen?": what the service is used for, that Android
allows locking only this way, that it doesn't read the screen, what you type or other apps, that it
collects and sends nothing, and that Android's Accessibility settings open next. Only *Allow* opens
them; *Not now* opens nothing and leaves a line, "The screen lock is off", whose tap shows the
disclosure again. With the service on, =allow lock= opens Android's settings at once, to turn it off.

** What to enter in Play Console

- *Is the app an accessibility tool?* No.
- *Why the app uses the AccessibilityService API:* App functionality. The purpose, ready to paste:

#+begin_example
Kolahal is a home screen app. Its accessibility service does one thing: it locks the screen when the user asks, with the lock command or the gesture they set to lock, such as a double-tap on the home screen. Android allows an app to lock the screen only through an accessibility service (performGlobalAction with GLOBAL_ACTION_LOCK_SCREEN).
#+end_example

- *What it does not do* (for a field that asks, or a reply to review):

#+begin_example
The service receives no accessibility events and cannot retrieve window content. It does not read the screen, what the user types or other apps, performs no action other than locking the screen, and collects and sends nothing. It is off until the user turns it on, after an in-app disclosure that says this and asks for consent.
#+end_example

- *Is personal or sensitive data collected or shared through accessibility capabilities?* No.
- *Video:* a link to the video below (YouTube, unlisted).

** The video

About thirty seconds, on a phone or the emulator with the Play build, the service off at the start
(turn it off in Android's accessibility settings). Play wants the full disclosure
readable, the consent flow, the non-consent flow and how to reach the disclosure again, and the
feature itself. Captions help where it isn't obvious.

1. Home: Kolahal is open. Type =allow lock= and press Enter. (0–4 s)
2. The disclosure: hold still on it for a few seconds, all of it on screen. (4–9 s)
3. Tap *Not now*: nothing opens; the log says "The screen lock is off / Tap to turn it on". Tap that
   line: the disclosure is back. (9–14 s)
4. Tap *Allow*: Android's Accessibility settings open. Tap Kolahal screen lock, turn it on and
   confirm Android's dialog. (14–22 s)
5. Back to home. Type =lock= and press Enter: the screen locks. (22–26 s)
6. Unlock. Double-tap empty space on home: the screen locks again. (26–30 s)

Record with =adb shell screenrecord= on the emulator (=-s emulator-5554=), or a phone's own screen
recorder.

** If Google refuses it

The rejection comes by email and in Play Console's Policy status, citing the AccessibilityService
API policy, usually with what was missing (the disclosure not seen in the video, the purpose not
clear, or a use Play doesn't accept). Answer the first two by fixing the video or the text and
sending the declaration again. If Play won't accept the use, take the lock out of the Play build
with two changes and nothing else in the code: in =app/build.gradle.kts=, the =play= build type's
=buildConfigField("boolean", "LOCK_SERVICE", "true")= becomes ="false"=, and
=app/src/play/AndroidManifest.xml= gets back, inside =<application>=,
=<service android:name=".gesture.LockService" tools:node="remove" />=. Every place that reads the
switch then hides =lock=, the double-tap choice, the quick action, the setup row and =allow lock=,
and R8 drops the service's code. Take the line out of the store listing and the declaration out
of Play Console, and put the row back in the table above.

Full description:

#+begin_example
Kolahal is a home screen made for touch and typing. Slide a thumb along the edge of the screen to any app, or type part of its name and press Enter. What you run stays in a log you can tap, swipe and long-press, and everything else is a short command away.

BY TOUCH
• An app selector on the screen's edge: slide your thumb through all your apps and lift to open one.
• Tap a line of the log for its main action; long-press for copy, share, open, run again and more.
• Swipe a line right to copy it, left to delete it.
• Gestures that run any command: a double-tap, a swipe down on the header, a swipe up on the prompt.
• Up to five widgets pages a swipe away, and a touch key bar while you type.
• Double-tap to lock the screen, with an optional accessibility service that only locks it.

BY TYPING
• Type any part of an app's name: the closest match sits right above your thumb.
• Instant launch opens an app as soon as the letters match only that app.
• With an empty prompt, your most-used apps are one tap away; pin favorites to keep them at the front.
• Aliases for anything you type often, app shortcuts, and every past command one ↑ away.

ALSO IN THE LOG
• App installs, updates and uninstalls show up as they happen.
• If you allow it, your notifications show up too, with media controls for what's playing.

LITTLE TOOLS BUILT IN
• A calculator with units and currencies: = 2^10, = 5 km in mi, = 100 usd in eur
• Web search with the engine you choose
• Call and text people by name: pick them once from your contacts
• To-dos, notes, reminders, timers and alarms, and your calendar's agenda
• Screen time per app, unlocks and daily limits
• Torch, volume, ring mode, Do Not Disturb, brightness and rotation from the prompt
• An RSS and Atom reader with unread counts and OPML import and export

WIDGETS
• Kolahal's own widgets for to-dos, shortcuts, your agenda, notes and what's playing, and other apps' widgets.
• Scripts fill widgets too: weather, a countdown, a habit streak and more.
• Each widget has its size, color and look, such as Bold, Shape or Poster.

SCRIPTS IN LUA OR JAVASCRIPT
• Run a script on a timer, at set times, or when something happens, such as an unlock or plugging in.
• Make a script into a command of your own that takes arguments.
• Pin a status line at the top, post a notification, fetch from the web.
• Start from 57 ready-made scripts, such as weather and live scores, in an editor with completion.
• Each script has a switch for each thing it uses, and runs sandboxed in a separate process.

A SCENE BEHIND IT
• On Android 13 and newer, 45 shaders draw on home: rain, snowfall, mountains in mist, a pond, an old CRT and more.
• Some are drawn from your screen time and unlocks, such as a candle that burns down through the day.
• Sliders change a shader and can follow the battery, the time of day or a value from a script.
• A scene can also be your phone's live wallpaper, and you can write shaders of your own.
• Off by default: a word in a notification can play a shader over the app you're in. It needs "Display over other apps".

MAKE IT YOURS
• Thirteen color themes, four in your wallpaper's colors, and a theme builder with a live preview
• Five monospace fonts, one you import, or your phone's own font
• Home on a character grid, like a terminal, with full-screen screens and a screensaver
• Settings that show your home screen underneath as you change them

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• No account, no ads, no analytics: Kolahal sends nothing to its developer.
• Everything stays on your phone, and backs up to one file that you keep.
• Crash reports stay on your phone until you choose to email one.
• Before you record or share the screen, one command replaces the text on home with random letters.

WORK PROFILE AND PRIVATE SPACE
• Their apps sit in the same list as yours, their icons badged, and you can pause or resume the work profile with a tap or a command.

The guide: mtwabpin.github.io/kolahal
The source code: github.com/mtwabpin/kolahal
#+end_example

** Taking the captures again

On the emulator only, never a phone, with =adb -s emulator-5554= for every command (a phone may be
attached too). They were taken on 24 November 2026 with the debug build of 0.5.17 (the full build,
package =com.mtwabpin.kolahal.debug=); what the Play build lacked then (the screen lock, calls in the log,
=update=) is kept out of them. With the Play build (=./gradlew -PplayApk :app:assemblePlay=) the
steps are the same with its package name.

The emulator: =sdk_gphone64_x86_64= (Android 16, API 36, Google Play image), 1080 × 2400 at 420 dpi
(=wm size=), gesture navigation, the host's GPU (shaders draw), animations off (the device tests
want them off). The stock apps give the suggestions and the selector's list.

The first captures were taken by hand with the steps below. All that show home were taken again
with =tools/site-captures.sh=, which holds the exact taps of every capture the website uses: its
=setup= does steps 1 to 3 (and a little more: favorites, the Water card, a few scripts), its
=restore= does step 6, and each capture is a function (the =play_*= functions for the store's own:
=play_home=, =play_answers=, =play_typing=, =play_selector=, =play_scenes=, =play_log=,
=play_scramble=, =play_sheets=). It sets the zone
=Asia/Dhaka= (UTC+6): a zone of whole hours keeps the world clocks' differences round, and the
script stops on a half-hour one.

* Each release on Play

Once the upload from the workflow is set up (below), the Release run puts the bundle on Play on the
testing track, with =docs/release/notes/X.Y.Z-play.txt= as its notes. With =PLAY_SEND_FOR_REVIEW=
=true= it rolls it out at once, which sends it for Google's review, and testers get it once it's
approved (with managed publishing on, once it's also published: Play Console → Publishing
overview). Otherwise it's a draft: open Play Console → Test and release → the track, check it and
roll it out. The run's summary says which version code and track, and which of the two. Without it, or when the upload fails (the run fails at its last
step, after the GitHub release is out):

1. The Release workflow keeps =kolahal-X.Y.Z-play.aab= with its run:
   =gh run download <run id> -n kolahal-X.Y.Z-play=.
2. Play Console → the track → Create new release → upload the bundle, and the =mapping.txt= beside
   it if Play doesn't show readable crash traces on its own. From 0.4.6 the bundle carries the
   native libraries' symbols itself (=ndk.debugSymbolLevel=); 0.4.4 and 0.4.5 went without, and Play
   asks for a symbol file: a zip of the bundle's own =base/lib/<abi>/*.so= files, as =<abi>/*.so=,
   uploaded in the App bundle explorer, serves.
3. Paste =docs/release/notes/X.Y.Z-play.txt= (500 characters at most) as the release notes.
   The first ones on Play introduced Kolahal and how to start; later ones list what changed since
   the last one on Play.
4. Roll out.

* Uploads from the workflow

=tools/play-upload.sh= (the Release workflow's last step) uploads the bundle through the Google Play
Developer API: one edit with the bundle and the release on the track, committed together. The
bundle carries its R8 mapping and native symbols, so nothing else goes up. It costs nothing: the
API, the Google Cloud project and its service account are free, with no billing account.

There is no key to keep or leak. GitHub gives each run an identity token; Google's Workload
Identity Federation trusts it only from =release.yml= run by a =v…= tag in this repository
(checked by the repository's and its owner's numeric IDs, so a repository given the same name later
gets nothing), and swaps it for half an hour as a service account. Play Console lets that account
release Kolahal to testing tracks, and nothing else.

Set up once:

1. A Google Cloud project with the Google Play Android Developer API, the IAM Service Account
   Credentials API and the Security Token Service API on.
2. A service account in it, with no roles in Cloud and no key.
3. Play Console → Users and permissions → Invite new users: the service account's email, no
   account permissions, and for Kolahal only *Release apps to testing tracks* (and *View app
   information*).
4. A workload identity pool (=github=) with an OIDC provider for
   =https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com=, mapping =google.subject=assertion.sub=,
   =attribute.repository_id=assertion.repository_id= (and the repository, ref and workflow_ref), with
   the condition
   #+begin_example
   assertion.repository_id == '<repository id>' && assertion.repository_owner_id == '<owner id>'
     && assertion.ref.startsWith('refs/tags/v')
     && assertion.workflow_ref.startsWith('mtwabpin/kolahal/.github/workflows/release.yml@refs/tags/v')
   #+end_example
   (=gh api repos/mtwabpin/kolahal= gives both IDs).
5. On the service account, =roles/iam.workloadIdentityUser= for
   =principalSet://iam.googleapis.com/projects/<project number>/locations/global/workloadIdentityPools/github/attribute.repository_id/<repository id>=.
6. Repository variables (not secrets; they are only names): =PLAY_WIF_PROVIDER=, the provider's
   full name (=projects/<number>/locations/global/workloadIdentityPools/github/providers/<id>=), and
   =PLAY_SERVICE_ACCOUNT=, its email. =PLAY_TRACK= only when the track isn't =alpha=, Play's first
   closed testing track. =PLAY_SEND_FOR_REVIEW= =true= to send each release for review instead of
   leaving a draft. Without the first two the step doesn't run.

When it fails, the run's log has Google's answer: no track by that name (it lists the ones there
are), a version code Play already has, a permission Play Console hasn't given yet (it can take a
while after the invitation), or, sending for review, other changes waiting in Play Console (a
rejected update, say) that Play won't send on its own: upload that release by hand.
