Kolahal 0.5.18, package `com.mtwabpin.kolahal`, Android 9 or newer. It installs over 0.5.17 and keeps your data and settings.

This release has no APKs: while Kolahal is in testing on Google Play, you install and update it from there. That is the Play build: no screen lock (`lock`, double-tap), no calls in the log and no `update`, and it finds people with Android's contact picker instead of reading your contacts.

## What's new

- **Deleting a notification's line dismisses the notification.** Swipe a notification's line away and the notification leaves Android's shade too, once Undo is no longer offered; **Undo** before that keeps it. **Delete line** in a line's menu dismisses it at once. Ongoing notifications, such as music or a call, stay. Clearing the log dismisses nothing: `notifs clear` is still the way to dismiss them all. **Settings → Log → Deleting a line dismisses its notification** (`notifs.dismiss-on-delete`) turns it off.
- **A script can say which apps' notifications it runs on.** `@on notification com.example.chat` runs the script only for that app's notifications, and several apps can follow the word, separated by spaces: `@on notification com.example.chat org.example.mail, unlock`. The script is not started for other apps. A script's sheet lists the apps under **Events**, each on a line of its own, an installed one with its icon. Without a package, `@on notification` runs for every app, as before. A shared script that names apps asks for `@requires 0.5.18`.
- **A warning when a script has the internet together with private data.** A script that can read your notifications, clipboard, apps, work profile, to-dos, notes, reminders, calendar, feeds or app usage, and can also use the internet, is able to send what it reads off the phone. Its sheet, its card and the dialog where you turn it on or install it now say so in one line, naming what it can read, and the same line follows `script on`, `script allow`, `script install` and `script access` in the log. The Scripts list marks such scripts and has a filter for them, **Internet and private data**. It is a warning only: nothing is blocked, and it goes when either side is switched off.
- **Album art as the Now playing card's background.** On the card's settings page (Arrange widgets, the card, **Settings**), **Album art as background** under What shows lets the track's cover fill the whole card, behind the words and the buttons, in every look and at every size. The card's own color lies over the picture so the track, the times and the seek bar stay readable. Without a cover the card is as before. It is off until you turn it on.
