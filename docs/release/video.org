#+TITLE: The preview video for Google Play
#+STARTUP: showall

A plan for Kolahal's preview video on its Google Play listing: what it shows, in which order, with
what on the phone, and how to record and put it together. Recorded on 24 November 2026 with
=tools/video/record.sh= ([[*Making it][Making it]]); the cut's real seconds are in =video.json=. Written on 24
November 2026 against branch 0.5.17; every command, script and setting in it was checked against
the guide (=site/src/content/docs/=) and the code of that day. Where a step could not be checked
without a device, [[*What is uncertain][What is uncertain]] says so.

It runs 298 seconds (4 min 58 s) in 16 chapters, and a short cut of 109 seconds keeps 10 of them.
Four chapters came out longer than planned once recorded (4, 5, 6 and 10: the steps of their
shots take longer than the plan's seconds); the shot tables keep the plan's timing.

* What it is for and what it must obey

The video goes in Play Console → Grow users → Store presence → Main store listing → Graphics →
Preview video, as a YouTube address. Play shows it behind the play button on the feature graphic
(=graphics/feature-graphic.png=), which a listing with a video must have. Play's rules, from
[[https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151][Preview assets]] and
[[https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/1078870][Add preview assets]]
(both opened on 24 November 2026):

| Rule                                                                        | What it means here                                                                                            |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| One YouTube video's address: no playlist or channel, no timecode in it      | paste the plain ~https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…~ address                                                   |
| Public or unlisted, never private; embedding allowed                        | Unlisted is enough                                                                                            |
| Ads off: monetization off, and no copyrighted content that brings ads back  | no music under Content ID: no music, or a track from YouTube's Audio Library                                  |
| Not age-restricted                                                          | nothing in it could be; on YouTube answer "No, it's not made for kids" and leave the age restriction off      |
| Landscape or portrait; a portrait video without black bars at its sides     | landscape, 16:9 ([[*The shape of the picture][below]])                                                       |
| No length limit, but only the first 30 seconds play by themselves, muted    | the first 30 seconds show the app at once and need no sound; captions carry everything                        |
| The real app early, within the first 10 seconds                             | the phone is on screen from the first frame                                                                   |
| At least 80% of it the app as people use it; few title screens and logos    | a 4-second opening and a 5-second closing; the phone shows the app in every other second                     |
| No people holding or touching the device                                    | no hands: touches show as Android's own dots ("Show taps")                                                    |
| No claims ("best", "#1", "top", "new", "free", "sale") and no calls to action ("download now", "install now", "try now") | the captions describe; the closing names the app and where it is, nothing more                     |
| Captions, for people who can't hear it and for places where it can't be heard | captions in the picture, and the same words as a caption track on YouTube                                   |

Play asks to avoid device imagery in screenshots and the feature graphic, because a device dates
quickly. The video shows the screen in a plain rounded frame, as the website does, with no buttons,
no camera and no make of phone.

*The Play build.* The listing is for the Play build, so the video shows only what it has: no screen
lock (no =lock=, no double-tap to lock), no calls in the log (no =calls=), no =update=, and no
contacts read by name. Record with the Play build installed (google-play.org → Taking the captures
again, step 1).

*No personal data.* The phone is an emulator with no Google account and only the invented data of
[[*The data to prepare before recording][The data to prepare]]: well-known cities, an invented
first name (Maya), public feeds, no people, no notifications, no messages. =scramble= isn't needed
and would hide the text the video is about. The log is cleared before each take, so no line from
an earlier take or from setting up shows.

* The shape of the picture

*Frame.* 16:9, mastered at 3840 × 2160 and published by YouTube at every size under it. A phone's
screen is tall and Play's player is wide, so the phone stands at the left and the captions sit at
the right, on a ground in the website's Scenes look. A portrait video would show the phone larger,
but the captions would have to cover the screen, and Play forbids black bars beside it.

- *The phone*: the recording at the frame's full height less a margin (about 1840 of 2160 pixels at
  4K), in a frame as the website draws one (=.w-phone= in =site/src/components/Ways.astro=): round
  corners, a thin edge in =--s-phone-edge=, the frame in =--s-phone=, and the one shadow the website
  allows, under the phone. Its left edge about a ninth of the way in.
- *Close-ups*: in chapters 1, 2 and 4 the prompt and the suggestions are what matters, and they are
  small at full height. There the phone grows to 1.5 times and slides up, so its lower 60% fills
  the frame's height and its top runs off the frame. It goes back to full size between chapters.
- *Captions*, in the right half: a section label (Instrument Sans, bold, upper case, letter-spaced,
  in =--s-muted=), a heading of a few words (Bricolage Grotesque, bold, tight, in =--s-ink=), and at
  most two lines of text under it (Instrument Sans, in =--s-ink-soft=). A command shows in
  JetBrains Mono on a code chip (=--s-code= with =--s-on-code=, dark in both themes, 14 px round at
  the website's scale). Each caption rises into place as it fades in, and stays at least 3 seconds,
  and longer for longer ones: about 15 characters a second.
- *No voice.* Play plays the first 30 seconds muted, and most people meet it so. The captions tell
  the whole story.
- *Sound*: none, or one quiet track from YouTube's Audio Library (one that needs no attribution),
  so YouTube puts no ads on it. No sound effects for taps.

*The ground and the chapters.* The ground is a scene from the app's own shaders, at the top of the
frame and fading into the ground's color, as at the top of the website's home page. It changes with
the parts of the video, sliding on as the next one slides in over it, and the tokens of the
captions change with it (=site/src/styles/tokens.css=: the light theme's for a light ground, the
dark theme's for a dark one):

| Chapters                                        | Ground                                                | Tokens        |
|-------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+---------------|
| Opening, 1 to 4: touch and typing               | =mountains= (=site/src/assets/scenes/mountains-wide.jpg=) | light         |
| 5 to 10: scripts and widgets                    | =pond= (=pond-wide.jpg=)                              | dark          |
| 11 and 12: shaders and their parameters         | =dunes= (=dunes-wide.jpg=)                            | light         |
| 13: the live wallpaper                          | =pond=                                                | dark          |
| 14: the TTY style                               | no scene: the plain =--s-code= color                  | dark          |
| Closing                                         | =mountains=                                           | light         |

The website pairs =mountains= with its light theme and =pond= with its dark one, and the video
keeps the pairs. A drift of the picture (a slow pan of a few percent over a chapter) is enough
movement: a moving ground beside a moving phone pulls the eye from the app.

The phone shows the app in the =rose-pine= theme, dark (the colors of Rosé Pine), as the store's
screenshots do, so the listing looks the same in its pictures and its video, and the phone sits well
on the website's colors. It keeps the app's own colors and font in every part. The widgets page is in
the app's Material 3 Expressive looks (Tonal, Bold, Split, Shape) from its first frame, never the
Plain one ([[*The data to prepare before recording][the data to prepare]]).

letters a second. "Enter" is the keyboard's Enter key. Each chapter starts from home with the
keyboard down and a cleared log unless it says otherwise. The captions are the exact words.

** 8. Pinning a script's line (18 s)

the pinned header. A script never pins itself: you do, with *Pin <name> to the top* on any line it
logged, the card's *pinned* pill or *Pinned at the top* switch, or =script pin <name>=. The real
example is =weather= (its runs without a city set "Tokyo: ☀️ 12°C"); the demo script =focus=
([[*The demo script][below]]) adds a bar, which no library script shows at a time you can choose.

** The demo script

Chapter 8 shows a pinned line with a bar. The library's lines with a bar (=aqi=, =cricket=,
=football=) depend on the air, or on a match being played at the time of the take, so the video uses
this short script instead. It is a working focus timer, not made-up data: =focus 2= starts a
two-minute session, and its line at the top says how much is left, with a bar that grows every 10
seconds. Checked against the API reference (=status(text, opts)=, =store=, =args=, =options=,
=event=):

#+begin_src lua
-- @title Focus
-- @desc A focus session as a line at the top of home: focus [<minutes>|stop]
-- @command
-- @usage [<minutes>|stop]
-- @option minutes 25 How long a session lasts, in minutes

local now = os.time()
local word = (args[1] or ""):lower()
local ends = tonumber(store.get("ends"))
local length = tonumber(store.get("length")) or 1500
local refresh = event and event.name == "refresh"

if word == "stop" then
  store.set("ends", nil)
  status(nil)
  return "Focus stopped"
end
if word ~= "" and not tonumber(word) then
  return "Usage: focus [<minutes>|stop]"
end

-- Typed with minutes, or with none while no session runs: a new session.
if not refresh and (word ~= "" or not ends or ends <= now) then
  local mins = tonumber(word) or tonumber(options.minutes) or 25
  if mins < 1 or mins > 240 then return "Minutes from 1 to 240" end
  length, ends = mins * 60, now + mins * 60
  store.set("ends", tostring(ends))
  store.set("length", tostring(length))
end

if not ends then
  status(nil)
  return
end
local left = ends - now
if left <= 0 then
  store.set("ends", nil)
  status("Focus done at " .. os.date("%H:%M", ends))
  return
end
status("Focus: " .. math.ceil(left / 60) .. " min left",
  { progress = 1 - left / length, refresh = 10 })
if not refresh then return "Focus until " .. os.date("%H:%M", ends) end
#+end_src

What it shows:

- Typed: one log line, "Focus until 17:44". Its long-press menu has *Pin focus to the top*,
  because the script calls =status=.
- Pinned: "Focus: 2 min left" in the pinned header, with a bar in the accent color under it. Home
  runs it again every 10 seconds while the line shows (=event.name= is ="refresh"=, which logs
  nothing), so the bar grows a twelfth at a time and the minutes count down.
- At the end: "Focus done at 17:44", without a bar, and the runs every 10 seconds stop.
- =focus stop= clears the line. A tap on the pinned line runs the script: during a session it logs
  the end time again; after one, it starts a new one.

It needs no access beyond *Pinned at the top*, which pinning turns on. It is written for the video
and isn't added to the library.

* How to record

** The device

An Android emulator with Android 15 or 16 (shaders need 13 or newer), a Google APIs image (no Play
Store, so no account is needed), 1080 × 2400 pixels at 420 dpi (a Pixel 8's size, as the store's
screenshots), no device frame ("skin"), the host's GPU for graphics (shaders are slow on the
software renderer), and its window shown. Gesture navigation, as most phones have now; the app
selector starts only in the middle of its strip, so it never takes the back gesture.

Every =adb= command goes to the emulator only: set =ANDROID_SERIAL= to its name (=emulator-5554=)
in the shell that records, so a phone on the same computer is never touched.

** Recording the screen

Two ways, either is fine:

- On the device: =adb shell screenrecord --bit-rate 20000000 /sdcard/07-scheduling.mp4=, then =adb pull=.
  A recording stops after 3 minutes (the default, and the most on older Android); one chapter per
  file stays well under it. If the encoder refuses 1080 × 2400, add =--size 720x1600= and check the
  text is still sharp, or use the other way.
- From the emulator: =adb emu screenrecord start --time-limit 180 <file>.webm= and
  =adb emu screenrecord stop=, or Extended controls → Record and playback. It records on the
  computer, so it needs nothing from the device's encoder.

Record each chapter as a file of its own, named by its id (=00-opening.mp4= to =15-closing.mp4=,
[[*Making it][Making it]]), and each take again until it is clean; keep 2 seconds of still screen at
both ends for the cuts. "Show taps" draws each touch as a dot in the recording itself.

** Typing by hand or by script

- *Typed text*: by script, for takes that come out the same each time. The on-screen keyboard stays
  open (tap the log first), and the text goes in one character at a time, so the suggestions change
  as they would:
  #+begin_src sh
  type() { s=$1; for ((i=0; i<${#s}; i++)); do c=${s:i:1}; [ "$c" = " " ] && c=%s; adb shell input text "'$c'"; sleep 0.25; done; }
  type "date tokyo london"; sleep 1; adb shell input keyevent KEYCODE_ENTER
  #+end_src
  The keyboard's keys don't light up this way; only the prompt shows the letters. That reads as
  typing in the video. Each =adb shell input= call takes a moment itself, so check the pace in the
  first take and change the =sleep=.
- *Taps, long-presses and straight swipes*: by script, with coordinates read once with Developer
  options → Pointer location: =input tap x y=, =input swipe x y x y 900= (a long-press: a swipe
  that stays put for 900 ms), =input swipe x1 y x2 y 250= (a swipe of a line).
- *The app selector (chapter 3)*: by hand, with the mouse in the emulator's window. Its path bends
  (along the edge, then off it, then along the list) and the speed matters (below about 100 dp a
  second it moves one app at a time); =adb shell input= starts a new process for each step, too slow
  for a smooth slide. Do several takes and keep the smoothest.
- *Sliders and dragging (chapters 10 and 12)*: by hand, or =input swipe= with a long duration
  (=input swipe x1 y x2 y 3000= drags a slider over 3 seconds).
- *The charger and the battery (chapters 7 and 12)*: the emulator's console, which needs no touch:
  =adb emu power ac on=, =adb emu power ac off=, =adb emu power capacity 62=.
- *What can't be scripted*: the network's answers (=weather=, =forecast= and the
  wallpaper preview's first frame take their own time; cut the wait), the real weather they show,
  the lock screen (it shows the system's clock), and Android's own screens, whose buttons move
  between Android versions (read their places before the take).

** What to check in each take

- Nothing personal: no account, no notification, no name but Maya.
- The Play build: no =lock=, no double-tap to lock, no =calls=, no =update=.
- No line left from an earlier take or from setting up; no "N new since you left"; no daily tip.
- The header's time runs on from the chapter before (set the clock again if not).
- Every network answer arrived, and no line is red unless the chapter means it.
- The keyboard is up or down as the shot says; the pinned header isn't hiding what the shot shows.
- Show taps is on, and each touch is where the caption says.
- A moving shader stands still after a minute without a touch unless the phone is charging: in
  chapters 11 to 13, touch the screen within the minute.
- The pace: a caption's words can be read in the time its shot takes.

* Putting it together

What each step needs; any tools that do it will serve. All of these exist for Linux: =ffmpeg= for
every video step, a headless Chrome for the caption cards (the website's own build tools already
use it), and, for anyone who prefers a timeline, Kdenlive, Shotcut or Blender's video editor.

1. *Trim.* Cut each chapter's file to its shots, frame-accurately (=ffmpeg -ss … -to …= with a
   re-encode), and remove waits (the network, the minute of chapter 7) with hard cuts.
2. *Caption cards.* One transparent PNG per caption, 3840 × 2160, made from an HTML page that uses
   the website's tokens and fonts (=site/src/styles/tokens.css=; Bricolage Grotesque, Instrument Sans
   and JetBrains Mono, all under the SIL Open Font License, from their =@fontsource= packages in
   =site/=), shot with headless Chrome (=--screenshot= with a transparent background). This keeps
   the type exactly as on the website. To draw text with =ffmpeg= instead, its =drawtext= needs the
   fonts as TTF or OTF files.
3. *Ground.* The scene pictures the website already has (=site/src/assets/scenes/mountains-wide.jpg=,
   =pond-wide.jpg=, =dunes-wide.jpg=), cut to 16:9, fading into the ground color (=--s-ground= of the
   theme the table above names), with a slow pan (=ffmpeg=: =zoompan= or a moving =crop=).
4. *Phone frame.* A PNG with the frame, its edge and its shadow, and a mask for the screen's round
   corners, made with the same HTML page. The recording is scaled into it and masked (=alphamerge=),
   then laid over the ground (=overlay=); for the close-ups of chapters 1, 2 and 4 it is scaled to
   1.5 times and moved up.
5. *Layers in time.* Each caption card over the frame for its seconds (=overlay= with
   ~enable='between(t,a,b)'~), faded in and out (=fade= with ~alpha=1~).
6. *Chapters.* Joined in order, with the phone held in place across the cut while its screen
   changes, the captions swap and the ground moves on ([[*Motion][Motion]]).
7. *Caption track.* The same words as an SRT file, one entry per caption with its times, for YouTube.
8. *Export.* H.264 (High), =yuv420p=, 30 frames a second (shaders draw at most 30), CRF about 18,
   =+faststart=, 3840 × 2160; an AAC track with the music or silence.
9. *Publish.* YouTube: unlisted, not made for kids, no age restriction, monetization off, embedding
   allowed, the SRT as English captions. Then its address in Play Console → Main store listing →
   Graphics → Preview video, and Save. Watch it on the listing with the sound off: it must make
   sense so.

The short cut is made from the same pieces: the same files, the same cards, the chapters of
its table.

** The commands

From the repository's root, with ffmpeg, Node.js and Chrome on the path and the website's packages
installed (=pnpm install --frozen-lockfile= in =site/=, for the fonts):

#+begin_src sh
tools/video/compose.sh full                 # build/video/kolahal-full.mp4 and kolahal-full.srt
tools/video/compose.sh short                # build/video/kolahal-short.mp4 and kolahal-short.srt
tools/video/compose.sh full --4k            # build/video/kolahal-full-2160p.mp4, for YouTube
tools/video/compose.sh full --only phone    # build/video/kolahal-full-phone.mp4
tools/video/compose.sh full --only framed   # build/video/kolahal-full-framed.webm
tools/video/compose.sh full --chapter 04-log   # one chapter's pieces in build/video/work/, to look at a recording
tools/video/compose.sh short --check        # checks video.json and writes the SRT only
#+end_src

Each writes the SRT beside the video, the same for every form. The forms:

- *The whole picture* (the default): the ground, the phone in its frame and the captions, at
  1920 × 1080, or 3840 × 2160 with =--4k=. This is the one for YouTube and Play: =--4k= for the
  upload, the default size to look at a change quickly.
- *=--only phone=*: the recordings alone, cut and joined with plain cuts, upright at their own
  1080 × 2400, with no ground, frame or captions. For places that want a portrait video (a post, a
  short), or as the source of another edit. A move of the bare screen would look like the app's
  own animation, so there is none.
- *=--only framed=*: the phone in its frame, with its round corners and shadow, on a transparent
  ground (VP9 with alpha in a WebM, which Kdenlive, Shotcut, Blender and browsers read). For laying
  the phone over another ground in an editor. It rises and lands as in the whole picture and its
  screen changes the same way, but it has no close-ups: those belong to the 16:9 frame.

Every form cuts between chapters in the same places, so they stay in step and the SRT's times hold
for all of them; the captions can travel beside a form that has none in the picture.

*How it is built, and its memory.* Each chapter is made by itself, into files under
=build/video/work/=: its phone clip, then its picture. The second around each cut, where two
chapters meet, is a job of its own that reads the end of one clip and the start of the next, and
the video is written by reading those files one after the other. Every move is worked out in the
video's own seconds, so a move that runs across a cut goes on unbroken in the next file. No job holds more than one chapter, ffmpeg runs one job at
a time with 4 threads (=VIDEO_THREADS=), still pictures are looped only for the seconds they show,
and where =systemd-run= is there each job runs under a memory cap (=VIDEO_MEMORY_MAX=, 6G), so a job
that grows is stopped alone. Each run prints the most memory a job used. A second run redoes only
what changed. (One filter graph over every chapter at once, the first way it was built, held the
decoded frames of the chapters still waiting their turn and grew past 15 GiB on the framed form:
keep each job to one chapter.)

** Recording: =tools/video/record.sh=

The recordings are made by a script on the emulator, as the website's pictures are
(=tools/site-captures.sh=, whose helpers and state it shares). Each chapter is a function with its
exact taps, typing and waits, so the whole recording can be made again in another theme or after

#+begin_src sh

tools/video/record.sh 04-log 05-scripts    # chapters, by id, each as a new take

tools/video/record.sh restore              # the emulator as it was, the app's data cleared

*The state* (=setup=): the capture script's (=tools/site-captures.sh setup=): Rosé Pine dark, the
header at the very top (=header.below-bar off=, the status bar hidden), the built-in =maps= off, the
selector's strip on, idle suggestions, no tips, no fresh screen by itself, the todos, an alarm
at 7:00, a public feed, the library scripts with their cities set before a first run (weather Tokyo,
forecast Lisbon, world clocks Tokyo, Nairobi, Lisbon), the Water and =focus= scripts typed into the
editor, Gboard's clipboard chip off, a zone of whole hours. Besides: instant launch off while
setting up (typed commands go in whole), the TTY style's own keys for chapter 14, and the widgets
page of the video: World clocks (Bold) and the forecast (Split), Todos (Split), Water (Shape) and
weather (Bold), so the cards placed in chapters 9 and 10 land under them. Each chapter makes what
it needs itself, off camera, and can be taken again alone: a cleared log, its own settings (instant
launch for apps from the third letter in chapter 1 only; Maps' data cleared there, as its start page
shows strangers' names and photos), the script, widget or pin an earlier take left removed.
Where this differs from [[*The data to prepare before recording][The data to prepare]] (Tonal there,
a feed and todos of its own), the script is what the recordings show.

*The takes.* =screenrecord= on the device, 1080 × 2400 at 20 Mbit/s (the text stays sharp through two
more encodes, and the shaders' motion doesn't smear), with Android's three animation scales at 1
(the app's own motion; moving shaders move) and "Show taps" on. Touches go in through the
emulator's console (=adb emu event send=), as the touchscreen's own events: "Show taps" draws no dot
for =input tap=, and a slide bends and moves at a finger's speed. Typed text goes in a letter at a
time, about four a second. Each take is made a constant 30 frames a second, with a sheet of a
frame a second beside it (=takes/<id>-<n>.jpg=) and the second of each step (=<id>-<n>.marks=), from
which the cuts in =video.json= are read. The clock is a zone of whole hours where it is about 17:00
at the start (=ZONE= sets one), picked again for chapters 11 to 13, whose scenes follow the hour.

*What the takes taught* (each is in the script's comments):

- A tap on the log opens the keyboard on touch-down and lifts on the suggestion that has just
  appeared under the finger: the log is tapped high, at y 700.
- A touch held still on the log is a long-press, which puts the last command back in the prompt,
  and a slow sideways one turns to the widgets page: the rain is touched with a small circle.
- Home doesn't close a sheet over Settings: Back does.
- The editor opens at a script's end, and a drag on the keyboard types: the keyboard is closed
  first, and four flings reach the top.
- A new widget lands in Plain while it loads: the cut goes from the picker to the page.
- A chapter that opens another app cuts away while its splash is up (Maps would show its sign-in).

*What to check in a take*, on its sheet: the first frames with a keyboard have its number row and
no clipboard chip; the header at the very top; no line from an earlier take or from setting up (a
history suggestion counts); nothing personal (no account, no notification, the machine's own zone
nowhere); in chapters 1, 2 and 4 what the captions speak of lies below the screen's top quarter
(the close-up hides it); every network answer arrived; each touch is where the caption says; the
pace (a beat of rest after each result); a still half second on each side of every cut; and, while
other work builds on the computer, no dropped or uneven frames.

whose file is missing becomes a plain screen with its name, and the run lists them, so the video

** Changing it: =tools/video/video.json=

- *The cuts.* Each recording starts with 2 seconds of still screen (=recording.lead=). With no cuts
  (=full.cuts= empty), a chapter is its seconds from there on. To take out a wait, or to start
  elsewhere, write the pieces to keep in the recording's own seconds, read in any player:
  ~"cuts": [{ "in": 2.4, "out": 14.4 }, { "in": 75, "out": 83 }]~. Their lengths add up to the
  chapter's =seconds=; the run says when they don't (it cuts the last piece short, or holds its
  last frame).
- *The short cut* follows by itself: its =short.segments= are in the full cut's seconds of a
  chapter (the shot list's "s" column), with ="chapter"= where a piece comes from another chapter.
- *A caption*: its words and its time from the chapter's start, under =captions= (=full= or =short=);
  it shows until the next one. =`word`= shows as code. The label and heading are the chapter's
  =head=; the opening's and closing's words its =card=. =--check= says which captions are on screen
  for less than 3 seconds or 15 characters a second.
- *A chapter's length*: its =seconds= (in =full= or =short=), then the times of its captions.
- *The close-ups*: =close= holds the seconds in which the phone is larger (=motion.close.scale=,
  1.5): it starts to grow at the first and is back at full size by the second.
- *The motion*: =motion=, below ([[*Motion][Motion]]).
- *The ground*: the chapter's =ground=, one of =grounds= (the scene's file and the tokens it takes).

** Motion

Every move is made of a few kinds, set once in =video.json= → =motion= and used everywhere
(=tools/video/motion.mjs=). Distances are pixels of a 1920 × 1080 frame (doubled at 4K), times are
seconds.

- *A spring* moves a thing to its new place fast at first, past it by a little, and back to rest:
  a weight on a spring, written by what it looks like. =overshoot= is how far past its place it
  goes, as a share of the move (0.03 is 3%; 0 goes straight in, critically damped); =settle= is
  the seconds until it stays within 1% of the move. A lower settle is a stiffer, quicker spring; a
  smaller overshoot a heavier, more damped one. Stiffness and damping (for a mass of 1) follow from
  the two, and =compose.sh short --check= prints them. A spring moved again before it rests adds
  the two moves, as a real one does. There are three:

  | Spring   | Overshoot | Settle | Stiffness | Damping | Moves                                         |
  |----------+-----------+--------+-----------+---------+-----------------------------------------------|
  | =phone=  | 3%        | 0.6 s  | 121       | 16.4    | the close-ups; the phone rising and landing   |
  | =words=  | 2%        | 0.5 s  | 169       | 20.3    | each part of a caption or card coming in      |
  | =ground= | 1%        | 1.1 s  | 14.5      | 6.3     | the ground sliding at a cut                   |

- *The exit* (=exit=): what leaves goes in 0.2 s, slow at first and faster at the end (the
  power 2 of the time), so it leaves faster than it came.
- *The stagger* (=stagger=, 0.08 s): a chapter's label, heading and first line, and a card's
  name, line and last line, come one after the other.

What each move does with them:

- *Close-ups* (=close=): the phone grows to =close.scale= (1.5) on the phone spring, scale and
  place together, about one point of its screen that stays where it is (=close.anchor=: half way
  across and the very bottom, as shares of the screen). It reaches 90% of the way in a quarter of a
  second and comes to rest by 0.6 s. It goes back the same way, starting early enough to be at
  full size by the =close= range's end. It holds still while close: a slow drift towards the line
  being read would move against the app's own scrolling and typing, and the recordings carry
  their own motion.
- *The phone arriving* (=phone=): at the start it fades in (=fade=, 0.3 s) from =rise= (60 px)
  below its place and a little larger (=grow=, 4%), and lands on the phone spring; its shadow is
  wider (=shadowGrow=, 12%) and lower (=shadowDrop=, 36 px) while it is lifted, and tightens under
  it as it lands.
- *Words* (=words=): each part comes up from =rise= (26 px) below on the words spring while it
  fades in (=fade=, 0.25 s), and leaves upwards by =exitRise= (12 px) with the exit. A chapter's
  words come in =delay= (0.12 s) after its cut, after the phone at a change of scene; at the start
  =opening= (0.3 s) after the phone sets off. A caption's line changes by the old line leaving
  upwards in the 0.2 s before the new one's time and the new one coming up at it, one after the
  other, so they never lie over each other; both keep at least the seconds =--check= counts.
- *A cut between chapters* (=change=, =ground=): the phone is the anchor and stays where it is,
  while everything else moves on, one second centred on the cut (=change.seconds=). The old words
  leave before the cut, the screen changes over =change.screen= (0.24 s) about it, and the new
  words come in after. The ground moves on by =ground.nudge= (40 px) on the ground spring, starting
  =ground.lead= (0.25 s) before the cut. At a change of scene (the ground changes) the old ground
  slides on by =ground.slide= (150 px) and the new one slides in by as much on the same spring,
  fading in over the old (=ground.fade=, 0.5 s), so the two move together; and the phone is lifted
  a little (=change.sceneKick=, 0.45 of a full lift) and lands again, highest on the cut, where
  its screen changes. =change.kick= does the same at every other cut (0: the phone holds still).
- *The cards*, at the opening and the end, come in like words: the name, then the line, then the
  last line. The closing card stays to the end.
- *The ground's drift* (=ground.pan=): 0.1% of the frame's width a second over each run of
  chapters on one ground, easing in after the cut where the run starts and out before the cut
  where it ends.

To make all of it calmer, raise the springs' =settle=; to make it livelier, lower it. Keep
overshoots to a few percent: past about 10% a thing visibly bounces back. Every piece whose moves
change is made again on the next run, and nothing else.

*What the close-ups need from the recordings.* A close-up shows the screen's lower three quarters
or so (at 1.5 times, its top quarter is off the frame): in chapters 1, 2 and 4 the prompt, the
suggestions, the keyboard and the newest lines of the log, all in the lower part, are what the
captions speak of. A recording in which the thing to read sits in the top quarter needs its
=close= range shortened, or =close.anchor= moved up (=[0.5, 0.75]= keeps the point three quarters
down in place; the phone's bottom then runs off the frame).

* What is uncertain

- *"Fresh command and longs"* is read as the =fresh= command and the log ("logs"), with long-press
  in it too. If it meant something else, chapter 4 is the one to change.
- *"App slider"* is the app selector. It is off by default, so a viewer needs Settings → App
  selector; the chapter's last caption says so.
- *Pinning* works as the words suggest, with two differences: a script never pins itself (the user
  pins it), and a pinned line takes turns with what's playing and the other pinned lines. Real
  scripts have lines to pin (=weather=, =countdown=, =charge-reminder=), so the demo script is there
  only for the bar.
- *Permissions*: a library script installs with everything it declares allowed; the choice comes
  after, in its sheet. (Get more scripts, which asks first, depends on the shared list of the day
  and isn't shown.) Internet and "Pinned at the top" have no =@uses= line: Kolahal reads them from
  the code.
- *Shaders need Android 13.* The emulator must have it; a viewer with Android 9 to 12 gets none of
  chapters 11 to 13, so the caption says "Android 13 or newer". The live wallpaper can't be a shader
  that changes text (=crt= and the like), which is why chapter 13 uses =pond=.
- *Not tried on a device* (no emulator was used for this plan): that =adb shell input text= drives
  the prompt as the keyboard does (suggestions, instant launch); that =clo= opens Clock on its third
  letter with the emulator's apps and no command in the way; which rows =ma= shows besides Maps;
  how soon the camels follow =adb emu power capacity= (the value comes from the battery's state; if
  nothing changes within a few seconds, go to the widgets page and back); the format of the place
  in =weather='s status line (wttr.in names it); whether the device's =screenrecord= takes
  1080 × 2400 on the emulator; and the exact names of Android's own wallpaper buttons.
- *=habit=* is left out of the widgets: its chart of dots needs some days of history, which a fresh
  emulator doesn't have. To show it, run =habit done= on the emulator each day for a week first.
- *The guide and the code differ on one small thing*: the guide writes the clean screen's line as
  "▴ N earlier lines"; the app shows "▴ N earlier lines · tap to show". The shot list uses the app's
  words.
