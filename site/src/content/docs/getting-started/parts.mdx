The guide uses a name for each part of Kolahal, such as *the pinned header*, *the suggestions* and *the key bar*.
This page shows each part, numbered on screenshots of the app. Then you know what each name means when you read about it.
The screenshots use the default theme, green.

## Home screen

From top to bottom, the home screen has the **header** (1–2), the **pinned header** (3), the **log** (4–9) and the
**prompt** (10–11). [Basics](/getting-started/basics/) explains each one.

<Annotated
	src={home}
	alt="Kolahal's home screen: the header with clock, date, alarm, open todos and battery, a pinned script's line under it, the log with four groups, and the prompt"
	marks={[[17.0, 5.3], [79.0, 5.3], [69.0, 8.1], [50.0, 30.0], [62.0, 71.0], [3.1, 79.0], [86.7, 75.6], [38.9, 83.6], [42.8, 86.1], [9.5, 97.7], [88.9, 97.7], [94.5, 60.0]]}
>

1. **Clock and date.** Tap to see your alarms.
2. **Header items**: the parts on the header's right side (here, the next alarm, open todos and the battery). Tap one to open it.
3. **Pinned header**: the line of a script that you pinned (here, the *countdown* script). Tap it to run the script. Long-press it for more.
4. **Empty log space.** Tap it to open the keyboard. Double-tap it to lock the screen (not in the Play build).
5. **A group**: a command and its output, with a gap above it.
6. **Coloured bar**: green next to commands, the script's colour next to a script's lines, and red next to errors.
7. **Time**, on the first line of each group.
8. **A command you typed.** Tap it to put it back in the prompt.
9. **Output.** Long-press any line to see its actions.
10. **Prompt** (`$`). Tap the `$` to open quick actions.
11. **Key bar button** (⌨): turns the key bar on or off.
12. **App selector handle**, on the edge of the screen, when the [app selector](#app-selector) is on.

</Annotated>

## While you type

While you type, the pinned header hides to give the suggestions more room. The key bar appears above the
keyboard.

<Annotated
	src={typing}
	alt="Typing ma at the prompt: the header, the log, the suggestion list with Maps highlighted at the bottom, the prompt and the key bar"
	marks={[[48.0, 4.1], [50.0, 32.8], [30.0, 59.6], [80.0, 75.0], [28.0, 82.6], [17.8, 89.9], [79.4, 89.9], [57.2, 96.9]]}
>

1. **The header**. It doesn't change while you type.
2. **The log.**
3. **Suggestions**: the apps, commands and settings that match what you typed.
4. **A command suggestion**, with its description.
5. **Best match**, highlighted at the bottom, nearest your thumb. Press Enter (⏎) to open it.
6. **The prompt**, with what you typed.
7. **Clear** (✕) empties the prompt.
8. **Key bar**: keys that the keyboard doesn't have (symbols, cursor, history), above the keyboard.

</Annotated>

## App selector

Turn it on in **Settings → App selector**. To use it:

1. Touch the edge of the screen where the handle is.
2. Slide along the edge to a letter.
3. Slide off the edge onto the list, to an app.
4. Lift your finger.

[Basics](/getting-started/basics/#app-selector) has the details.

**Sliding along the edge**

<Annotated
	src={selectorSlide}
	alt="Sliding along the right edge at G: the panel of apps on the left, Gmail and Google bright, the G bubble beside the thumb and the letters fanning out from the edge"

>

1. **Panel**: every app in one list. Favourites (★) come first or last, then the other apps from A to Z.
2. **Section letter**, in the panel's margin.
3. **The letter's apps**, bright. They start at the same height as your thumb.
4. **Other letters' apps**, faded.
5. **Bubble**: the current letter, large, next to your thumb.
6. **Wave**: the letters near the current one stick out, so you can see which way to go.
7. **Edge strip**: the part of the edge you slide on. Each letter has its own position on it.
8. **Home**, dimmed while the selector is open.

</Annotated>

**Picking from the list**

<Annotated
	src={selectorPick}

1. **The app at your thumb**, marked. Move up or down to pick another app. Lift your finger to open it.
2. **Hold fill**: if you hold still, the row fills with colour. When it's full, the app opens.
3. **The rows next to it**, so you can see where you are. Every row stays the same size.

**Left open** (lift on the edge)

1. **The list, left open**. It is almost as wide as the screen, so your thumb can reach every row. Scroll it, tap an app, or long-press an app to see its actions.
2. **Space at the start of the list**, so the apps for the letter you tapped stay next to it.
3. **Edge strip**: you can slide along it again from here.

## Scripts tab

The script manager, with a card for each script. See [Scripting](/scripting/overview/).

<Annotated
	src={scripts}
	alt="The Scripts tab: filter chips, two tinted cards for scripts that are on, one with its details open, and a faded card for a script that is off"
	marks={[[19.0, 35.6], [74.5, 8.8], [93.0, 11.5], [59.0, 41.0], [8.7, 48.1], [38.0, 51.5], [62.7, 48.1], [75.5, 48.1], [88.0, 47.9], [82.2, 53.9], [79.2, 57.3], [34.3, 75.2], [25.5, 78.2], [63.4, 78.2], [47.0, 88.7], [80.0, 91.6]]}
>

1. **Filter chips**: commands, timers, events, pinned, failed, off, differs from the library and
   more. Each chip shows a count.
2. **Search** scripts by name.
3. **All details**: opens or closes the details of every card.
4. **A script that's on**: its card is tinted with the theme's colour.
5. **Dot**: on, failed (red) or off (grey).
6. **Title and file name.**
7. **Run**, for a command script.
8. **Details arrow**: shows or hides the details of this card.
9. **On/off switch.**
10. **Summary**: how the script runs, when it last ran, and "edited" if you changed it.
11. **Its status line**, as it is now.
12. **Pills**: what the script may use. A pill is filled when on and faded when off. Tap a pill to turn it on or off.
    *pinned* puts the script's status line in the pinned header.
13. **Compare** your copy with the library version.
14. **Reset to library version.**
15. **A script that's off**: its card is faded.
16. **Add script**: write a new script, or add one from the library, from other people or from a file.

</Annotated>

## Script editor

To open the editor, run `script edit <name>`, or tap **Edit** in a script's sheet. See [Editor](/scripting/editor/).

<Annotated
	src={editor}
	alt="The script editor: the file name and the find, help, save, run and close buttons at the top, numbered lines of code, and the symbol bar"
	marks={[[11.1, 8.1], [27.7, 8.1], [39.6, 8.1], [54.7, 8.1], [71.8, 8.1], [90.0, 8.1], [3.4, 38.7], [11.7, 95.4], [70.0, 95.4]]}
>

1. **File name**. It shows `*` while there are unsaved changes.
2. **Find** (and replace).
3. **[?]**: a short summary of the whole scripting API.
4. **[save]**
5. **[run]**: saves the script, then runs it. Its output appears under the code. Long-press to run it with arguments, or as one of its events.
6. **[close]**: saves and closes the editor.
7. **Line numbers.**
8. **Undo and redo.**
9. **Symbol bar**: the brackets and symbols code needs, above the keyboard.

</Annotated>
