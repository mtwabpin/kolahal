[Kolahal](/) is a terminal style launcher for Android, optimized for touch and extensible with scripts and shaders. Version 0.5.17 is mostly about the two sides of home: shaders over the log, some of them drawn from how you use the phone, and new cards on the widgets page. Home can also be drawn on a character grid, and a few commands moved. The [release notes](https://github.com/mtwabpin/kolahal/releases) have every change.

## Shaders over home

Why shaders? Because home is the screen you come back to all day, and it seemed a pity for it to be only useful. A pond under a low moon, a candle that burns down with your day: none of it opens an app any faster. It is one of those ideas whose whole job is to make a small corner of life more beautiful.

On Android 13 and newer, a shader (a small program the graphics chip runs for every pixel) can be drawn over home and its full-screen screens. 45 come with Kolahal: some change how the text looks (an old CRT, e-paper, graph paper), others are scenes behind it (rain, snowfall, mountains, a pond, northern lights), and fourteen play once and go.

```sh title="At the prompt"
shader rain
```

`shader` alone lists them, and the **Shaders** tab, beside Settings, Scripts and Feeds, has them with previews and a search; `shaders` opens it. `shader off`, or two fingers held still on the screen for a second, always turns one off. A scene brings its own ground, so your wallpaper doesn't show through the log while it is on. Moving ones stand still in battery saver and after a minute without a touch.

## Scenes of how you use the phone

Eight shaders draw your screen time and unlocks. `candle` and `hourglass` run down as today's screen time is spent, against a usual day (the average of the six days before); `seismograph` and `day-ring` trace today; `windows`, `record`, `lanterns` and `kintsugi` show the week. Screen time needs usage access:

```sh title="At the prompt"
allow usage
shader candle
```

None of it leaves the phone. A shader has no network and no files: it gets numbers and draws them, and it can't send or save anything. [Privacy](/reference/privacy/) says what each permission is for.

## A shader as your wallpaper

```sh title="At the prompt"
wallpaper shader pond
```

This opens Android's own preview, where you set the shader as the live wallpaper of the home screen, the lock screen or both, behind any launcher. On the Shaders tab it is **Set as wallpaper** in a shader's details. The wallpaper keeps your parameters, follows the same values as on home, and moves only while you can see it. Shaders that change the text, such as `crt`, can't be a wallpaper. `wallpaper` alone says what your wallpaper is now and lists what it can become.

## Parameters that follow a value

Some shaders have parameters, such as the amount of snow: sliders in a shader's details, or `shader set snowfall amount 1.5`. A parameter can follow a value instead: the battery, the time of day, your unlocks, your screen time, or a value a script gives. With the library's **weather** script it rains on home as it rains outside:

```sh title="At the prompt"
script install weather
shader rain
shader follow rain amount weather.rain
```

A value can go through a formula (`shader follow dunes camels battery / 20`), and `shader values` lists what there is to follow.

## Shaders that play once

Why one-offs? A scene is there all day, but some things happen once. A message from someone you were waiting to hear from is worth a few seconds of hearts, and then they should go.

Fourteen shaders play once over home for a few seconds, drawn over everything on the screen, a scene included. `hearts` comes in seven motions: rising, a fountain, falling petals, a stream up the edge, poppers from the corners, fireworks and a bounce. Its hearts come in eight styles, from glossy and flat to neon and halftone (`shader set hearts style neon`). `pulse` runs light around the edge of the screen, and six more are made of light too: a comet, the edge breathing, a scan line, a spill from the top, a heartbeat trace and northern lights.

```sh title="At the prompt"
shader play hearts-fountain
shader words hearts picnic
```

The second line makes `hearts` play when a notification has the word "picnic"; in a one-off's details it is **Plays on a notification**, and it needs notification access.

## Writing your own

A shader is a `.agsl` file, in Android's version of GLSL. Six commented templates of 30 to 50 lines are the quickest start:

```sh title="At the prompt"
shader save template-tint
shader edit my-tint
```

**Save** in the editor checks it at once and names the line that fails. **Add shader** on the Shaders tab does the same with buttons and imports `.agsl` files. `@fps` and `@settles` in the header keep a moving shader cheap, and a one-off of yours plays over other apps once its header says it suits them. `shader get` lists the shaders shared in [kolahal-scripts](https://github.com/mtwabpin/kolahal-scripts), each reviewed first. More on the [shaders page](/scripting/shaders/).

## Widgets

Why more cards? Because the widgets page is the side of home you read without typing, and some things read best as what they are: a match as two sides and a score, the evening as the sun low on its arc, a parcel as a point on its way.

Scripts get new kinds of card for that. `versus` shows two sides, the score and a bar between them, such as possession; `sky` shows the sun on its arc or the moon in its phase, and Kolahal moves it through the day without running the script. A progress card can be a gauge, rings inside each other, or stages on a line for a parcel or a flight. A number can have its recent values as a line behind it, or one of Kolahal's scenes, and a countdown can be dots, one going out each day, or a calendar page. In the library, `football` and `cricket` show the score on a versus card, `aqi` is a gauge with the scale's zones, and `sun` has the sky.

## Daily limits

The scenes above show the day as it goes; a limit gives its figures a line to cross. It changes nothing else: no notification, nothing blocked, only the color of a number.

Set one for screen time (in minutes) and one for unlocks in **Settings → Header → Daily limits**. At the limit, today's figure turns the theme's warning color, and from one and a half times the limit its error color: in the header's unlocks, `usage`, `unlocks`, `top` and `fetch`, with a line that says how far over it is. The library's Screen time card takes the color too.

```sh title="At the prompt"
config set usage.unlock-limit 60
unlocks 7
```

Without usage access, the page says so and has an **Allow** button in place of the limits. Other options that need an access now do the same, such as a one-off's words without notification access.

## Home on a character grid

Why a TTY style? Because Kolahal is a terminal at heart, and now it can look like one all the way down. It does nothing a launcher needs. It is for the people who smile at a screen made only of characters on a grid, and it is there for fun.

```sh title="At the prompt"
config set home.style tty
```

Home's log, header and prompt are then drawn on a strict grid: one font size, whole lines, no pictures. `tty.suggestions`, `tty.selector`, `tty.keybar`, `tty.quick-actions` and `tty.menu` put the other parts on it too (`config` only), and `config set home.style gui` goes back.

Seven commands then take the whole screen until a tap, Back or a key: `matrix`, `pipes`, `sl`, `clock`, `top`, `decode` and `boot`. In the default style these names stay plain words; `screen top` opens one in either style. **Settings → Look & feel → Screensaver** opens `matrix`, `clock`, `pipes` or `decode` after 30 seconds to 60 minutes without a touch; the default style offers `decode`, which scrambles home's own lines where they are.

## scramble, for screen recordings

```sh title="At the prompt"
scramble
```

Every line of the log keeps its place, its length and its colors, but each letter and digit is replaced and pictures show as plain blocks, until `scramble off`. Nothing stored changes, and a tap works on the real line. What you type, the header's figures and other screens such as Settings stay readable: [Screen recordings](/reference/privacy/#screen-recordings) has the list.

## Commands that moved

Kolahal is in testing, and this release changed several commands without keeping their old names. If an alias, a gesture or a shortcut of yours runs one of the old ones, it needs the new one:

| Before | Now |
| --- | --- |
| `usage-access` | `allow usage` |
| `notifications` | `allow send-notifications` |
| `notifs enable` | `allow read-notifications` |
| `dnd access` | `allow dnd` |
| `brightness access` | `allow settings` |
| `remind exact` | `allow alarms` |
| `battery` | `allow battery` |
| `agenda --allow` | `allow calendar` |
| `contacts --allow` | `allow contacts` (full build only) |
| `calls --allow` | `allow calls` (full build only) |
| `accessibility` | `allow lock` (full build only) |
| `usage-summary` | `usage summary`, `usage week`, `usage month`, `usage year`, `usage full` |
| `wallpaper match` | `wallpaper theme` |
| `screen.bars` `show`, `hide`, `immersive` | `screen.hide-bars` `none`, `status`, `both` (**Hide system bars** in Settings) |

`--settings` after `agenda`, `calls` and `contacts` is gone too, `notifications` is now another name of `notifs`, and your `screen.bars` setting carries over. `allow` alone lists every permission, each marked allowed or not; a tap asks for it. `widget` acts and `widgets` opens the screen, as with `script` and `scripts`: `widget add`, `widget ls`, `widget page`.

`screen`, `shader` and `scramble`, and in the TTY style the seven screens' names, are now Kolahal's: a line that starts with one runs the command, not an alias, a script or an app. A script with one of these names still runs with `script run <name>`, and `script mv <name> <new-name>` renames it; **Settings → Commands** turns a command off.

## For people who write scripts

- `skill.md`, typed in full, copies a guide for an AI assistant on writing Kolahal scripts and shaders.
- The new cards are a script's to show: `versus` and `sky`, `style = "gauge"` or `"rings"`, `stages`, a number's `points` or `scene`, and the countdown styles `dots` and `page`.
- The editor's reference names every widget kind, style and field, each with an example, and **Home style** under a log example shows it as the GUI or the TTY style draws it.
- `tty.screen` draws a full-screen screen of your own on the grid. Start from the library's `template-screen` (or `template-screen-js`), or read Kolahal's own screens as scripts, such as `tty-matrix`. In the default style such a script is marked **TTY style only**.
- `shader.give` gives a value for shaders to follow, as `weather.rain` does; `shader.play` plays a one-off shader.
- A source of shared scripts and shaders is asked for its own version's list first, so scripts for a version can be shared before it is released.

- **Settings → About → Default home app** says whether Kolahal is your home app, and a tap makes it so or opens Android's page to choose another.
- Tap and hold a number setting, or a shader's parameter, to type its value.
- Among the fixes: a sheet or a dialog no longer brings the status bar back while **Hide system bars** is on, a rare crash in lists is gone, a second weather card for another city no longer changes the pinned weather line, and countdowns change their date at local midnight, not at midnight UTC.

## Getting it

Kolahal is in testing on Google Play, where you install and update it; this release has no APKs on GitHub.

1. Join the group [groups.google.com/g/kolahal](https://groups.google.com/g/kolahal) with the Google account your phone's Play Store uses.
2. Open [play.google.com/apps/testing/com.mtwabpin.kolahal](https://play.google.com/apps/testing/com.mtwabpin.kolahal) with the same account and tap **Become a tester**.
3. Install Kolahal from its [Play Store page](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mtwabpin.kolahal), where it is **Kolahal - Terminal Launcher**. It can take a few minutes to appear there after step 2.

It needs Android 9 or newer, and installs over 0.5.16 with your data and settings. Google Play releases Kolahal to everyone only after at least 12 testers keep it for 14 days in a row, so please keep it installed that long; [Test on Google Play](/getting-started/play-testing/) has the details. The Play build leaves out the screen lock, calls in the log and `update`, and finds people with Android's contact picker instead of reading your contacts.

Kolahal is free software under the GPL v3, and its [source is on GitHub](https://github.com/mtwabpin/kolahal). It is developed with AI assistance and the developers review every change except the tests; the [README](https://github.com/mtwabpin/kolahal) says where the review of the codebase stands. Keep a [backup](/guides/backup/), and [report](https://github.com/mtwabpin/kolahal/issues/new/choose) what breaks.
