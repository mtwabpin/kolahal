Kolahal can show the same home-screen widgets as any other launcher: clocks, weather, calendar,
music, notes. The widgets are on a separate **widgets page** beside the terminal, so they take no space from it.
The page also has [Kolahal's own widgets](#kolahals-own-widgets), such as your todos and what is
playing, and [widgets that scripts fill](#widgets-from-scripts).

## The widgets page

To open the widgets page, swipe sideways on the home screen. Start the swipe on the header, on empty space or on the prompt.
A swipe that starts on a log line copies or deletes that line instead. A swipe toward the page always copies: with the page on the right, a swipe left copies a line and a swipe right deletes it.

Other ways to open it:

- Run `widget page`, or tap **Widgets page** in the quick actions (tap `$` on the prompt). You can also set a [gesture](/guides/gestures/) to `widget page`.
- With TalkBack or Switch Access, use the **Widgets** action on home. On the widgets page, the **Log** action goes back.

- **+** (add a widget) and **✎** (edit your widgets) are at the top of the page.
- Back or Home takes you back to the terminal.
- `widgets.page` sets the side of the page: `left` (the default) or `right`. `off` turns the page off.

<Cards items={[{ src: widgetsPage, alt: "The widgets page: the + and pencil buttons at the top, then weather and battery cards, todos and a habit streak, and a row of shortcuts", width: 380 }]} />

The page can show your phone's wallpaper (the one you set in Android) behind the widgets.
**Settings → Widgets → Transparency** (`widgets.wallpaper`) sets how much of it shows:

- 0 shows none of it. This is the default.
- 100 shows all of it, with none of the theme's color over it.

The log has its own setting for this (`log.wallpaper`, under **Settings → Log**). So each page can show
more or less of the wallpaper.

When the [app selector](/getting-started/parts/#app-selector) is on (`selector.rail`), it is on the widgets page
too. Its strip is in the same place and uses the same settings as on the log. Its list uses the whole page,
and while it is open, the widgets fade out behind it.

You can make home open on the widgets page. It needs the [app selector](/getting-started/parts/#app-selector)
first: there it is your way to open apps. Turn it on in **Settings → App selector** (`selector.rail`), then
**Settings → Widgets → Start on the widgets page** (`widgets.start`). Then:

- Back and Home take you to the widgets page. Swipe sideways to reach the log.
- If you turn the app selector off, home opens on the log again. Turn the selector back on and home opens on the widgets page again.
- If you type on a hardware keyboard, Kolahal switches to the log and puts the text in the prompt.
- **Open the keyboard on Home** opens the keyboard when you reach the log.
- The page has no add and edit buttons. Long-press a widget to arrange widgets, or use `widget add`.

## Add a widget

You can add a widget in three ways:

- Tap **+** at the top of the widgets page.
- Open **Settings → Widgets → Arrange widgets** (or run `widgets`) and tap **Add widget**.
- Run `widget add` to go straight to the picker.

The picker lists every installed widget, grouped by app. It shows each widget's preview and default size
(`4×2` and so on). Tap a widget to add it:

1. The first time, Android asks if Kolahal may create widgets. Allow it. Tick "always" so Android doesn't ask
   again.
2. Some widgets have a setup screen, for example to pick a city, a calendar or a note. It opens next.
   If you leave it before you finish, the widget isn't added. To change it later, see
   [Widget settings](#change-a-widgets-settings).

## Kolahal's own widgets

Kolahal has seven widgets of its own. They show what Kolahal knows itself: your todos, your
reminders, what is playing. Kolahal draws them as cards, in the theme's colors.

- The picker lists them first, in the **Kolahal** group.
- They are live. A card changes when its data changes. No script runs for them, and they have no
  setup screen.
- Each is placed once. After that its row in the picker says **Added**.
- A long-press on a card opens **Arrange widgets**. There you move and resize it like any other
  widget, and choose its [look](#looks).

| Widget | It shows | A tap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Battery and network** | the battery's level in a ring, and the network | opens Android's battery screen |
| **Shortcuts** | up to 8 buttons, for commands you pick | on a button: runs its command |
| **Now playing** | the track with its cover, the artist and the app, with previous, play or pause, and next | on a button: does that. On the bar: moves in the track. On the card: opens the player |
| **Todos** | your open todos, each with a checkbox | on a row: marks the todo done. On "+2 more": runs `todo` |
| **Agenda** | your calendar's events for today and the next days | on an event: opens it in your calendar app. On the card: runs `agenda` |
| **Reminders** | the time to the next reminder, with its text, and the reminders after it | runs `reminders` |
| **Notes** | your newest note, or the newest three in a taller card | runs `notes`. In the **Stack** look, on +: starts a new note |

### Todos

The card lists your open [todos](/guides/productivity/#todos), the oldest first.

- Tap a row to mark its todo done. The row stays where it is, ticked, until you leave the widgets
  page. So a tap by mistake is easy to undo: tap the row again.
- A todo that you add with `todo buy milk` shows on the card at once.
- A card with more todos than fit says how many more there are ("+2 more"). Tap that line, or the
  name over a large card's rows, for the whole list. A tap between rows does nothing.
- In the **Next** look the card puts the next todo first, large, with a check to tap. A ring
  fills as todos are ticked ("3/7"). Tap the check and the todo after it comes up. **Undo** brings
  back the one you ticked last, until you leave the widgets page. A 4×1 card shows one todo at a
  time with "then" and the one after it; a 4×2 card shows the next ones under it as rows.

### Reminders and Notes

- **Reminders** shows the time to your next [reminder](/guides/productivity/#reminders) in large
  type ("42 min"), with its text, when it is due and how it repeats. The reminders after it are
  rows, each with its text and when it is due. The time counts down by the minute.
- **Notes** shows your newest [note](/guides/productivity/#notes). A taller card shows the newest
  three, each on one line.
- In the **Next** look, **Reminders** shows the time to the next reminder in a pill ("in 42 min")
  that empties in the last hour before it, and the reminders after it as points on a line. The
  pill moves by the minute.
- In the **Stack** look, **Notes** shows your newest note on a stack of sheets, one for each other
  note. Tap **+** to write a new note: home turns to the log with `note ` in the prompt and the
  keyboard open. Type the note and press Enter.

### Shortcuts

**Shortcuts** is a card of buttons for commands that you choose: `torch`, `dnd`, `todo`, `weather`,
`open maps`. A tap on a button runs its command, as if you had typed it and pressed Enter.

A new Shortcuts card has no buttons. It says "No shortcuts yet. Tap to add commands."

To edit your shortcuts:

1. Open the **Shortcuts** sheet in one of two ways:
   - While the card has no buttons, tap it anywhere. A tap beside its buttons does nothing.
   - Or open **Arrange widgets** (long-press the card, or **Settings → Widgets → Arrange
     widgets**), select the Shortcuts widget, and tap **Edit shortcuts** in the toolbar at the
     bottom.
2. Tap **Add shortcut**, and fill in the boxes:
   - **Command**: what you would type in the terminal, such as `torch`. While you type its first
     word, the commands that start so are offered under the box. Tap one to take it.
   - **Label**: what the button says. If you leave it empty, the button says the command.
   - **Icon**: if you like, an icon name such as `torch` or `pin` (the [scripting guide](/scripting/api/#icons) lists them), or an emoji.
3. Tap **Add**.

In the sheet, each shortcut has its own row:

- Tap a row to change that shortcut.
- The up and down arrows move it earlier or later. The buttons on the card are in this order.
- The bin removes it.

Each change is saved at once, and the card follows.

- A command can be up to 200 characters, a label up to 24, and an icon is a name or up to 4 characters.
- The card has up to 8 buttons. A small card shows the first ones only, and never makes a button
  smaller. So put the important ones first.
- The command can be any command, also one such as `torch` or `dnd` that a
  [script](/scripting/overview/) may not run. You chose it yourself.
- A script can't change your shortcuts.
- Your shortcuts are in a [backup](/guides/backup/). After an import, the result lists each
  shortcut that the file brought.

### Now playing

The card shows what plays in your music or podcast app: the title, the artist and the app, and a
bar for the position. When the app gives the album's cover, it fills the play and pause button.

- The three buttons are previous, play or pause, and next. A narrow card has fewer buttons: play
  or pause is always there.
- When the app can move within a track, drag the bar to go to another place in it, or tap the
  bar, at any size. With TalkBack, the bar is a slider that says where the track is, such as
  "1:12 of 3:05".
- A tap elsewhere on the card opens the app that plays.
- While something is paused, the card keeps it, however long ago you paused it. So you can start
  it again from here.
- When the app is closed, the card shows it with its last track, under "Last played", and a
  **Play** button that starts it again. On Android 12 and older, or if Android can't pass Play to
  that app, the app opens instead. After Kolahal restarts, the card still knows the app, but not the
  track. If nothing has played yet, the card says "Nothing playing".
- It needs [notification access](/guides/media-and-controls/#media), as the header's line for
  what is playing does. Without it, the card says "Allow notification access", and a tap asks
  for it.

### Agenda

The card shows the events of your phone's calendar from today, under "Today", "Tomorrow" and the
date. By default (Auto) it looks up to 14 days ahead and shows as many events as the card fits.
To show only a few days, choose 1, 3, 7 or 14 under **Days** when you tap the card in **Arrange
widgets**, or in **Settings → Widgets → Agenda days**. The [`agenda`](/guides/productivity/#agenda)
command reads the same calendar.

- Each event has its time in front. An all-day event says "all day" and comes first in its day.
  One that goes on says "now".
- A tap on an event opens it in your calendar app. A tap elsewhere runs `agenda`.
- An event that has ended leaves the card by itself.
- In the **Now** [look](#looks), the card answers what you are in, how long you have, and what is
  next: the event on now with a bar that fills as it goes, "free 1h 30" until the next event, and
  that event with its time and place. With nothing on now, it shows the free time until the next
  one. All-day events are never "now". A 2×2 or larger card shows the day, each event with its
  length and the free time between them.
- With no event in these days, the card says so: "No events in the next 14 days".
- It needs the calendar permission. Without it, the card says "Allow calendar access", and a tap
  asks for it.
- Kolahal reads the calendar when the widgets page comes into view, and every 15 minutes while you
  stay there.

## Widgets from scripts

A [script](/scripting/overview/#widgets) can have a widget too: a card that Kolahal draws from what the
script tells it. For example the weather, a feed's headlines, or your unlocks of the last 7 days.

- The picker lists these in the **Kolahal** group, after Kolahal's own: a row for each of your scripts
  that has a widget, with its kind and the size it starts with. Many
  [library scripts](/scripting/library/#widgets) have one. To make your own, start from a
  [widget template](/scripting/library/#templates).
- A script's [status line](/scripting/overview/#status-lines) is not a widget. It shows at the top
  of home.
- You can place a script's widget more than once ([below](#the-same-widget-more-than-once)).
- The card is drawn in the theme's colors.
- The card's labels start with a capital, whatever the script wrote. Units, versions and names
  such as "iOS" stay as written ([Capitals in a widget](/scripting/api/#capitals-in-a-widget)).
- A tap does what the script chose: it runs the script again, opens a link, or refreshes the card.
- A long-press opens **Arrange widgets**. There you move and resize the card like any other widget,
  and choose its [look](#looks). It can be as small as a quarter of the page wide.
- Under old content, the card says its age ("2 h ago"). After a run that failed, it says "Failed 10:42"
  and keeps what it had.

### The same widget more than once

A script's widget can be on the page several times, each with its own settings: the weather for
two cities, a countdown to two days.

1. Place the script's widget as usual. Its row in the picker then says
   **Added · tap to add another**.
2. Tap that row. One more widget is added. If the script has options, its **Options** sheet opens.
3. Set what this widget should show: its city, its day. These are the script's
   [options](/scripting/overview/#options).

To change a widget's options later:

1. Open **Settings → Widgets → Arrange widgets**.
2. Tap the script's card.
3. Tap **Options** in the toolbar at the bottom. It shows only for a script with options.

What to know:

- A script can have 6 widgets at most. Then its row in the picker says **Added · 6 of 6**.
- A further widget follows the script's own options until you set one for that widget. After
  that it keeps its own value for that option.
- The first widget has no options of its own. Its **Options** are the script's, the same as in the
  script's sheet and `script set`. A change in the first widget's **Options** sheet also runs the
  further widgets that follow it.
- The script's timer fills the first widget only. A further widget runs the script for itself:
  when you place it, when you look at it and its content is old, when you tap it, and when you
  change an option it reads: in its **Options** sheet, in the script's sheet on the Scripts screen,
  or with `script set`. So more widgets don't run anything more in the background.
- If you removed the first widget and a further one is still placed, a tap on the row brings the
  first widget back. The timer fills only the first.
- Removing a widget deletes what it showed and its own options. The script and its other widgets
  stay.
- Kolahal's own widgets are placed once.

## Arrange and resize

The page is a grid twelve columns wide. A widget can sit anywhere on it: beside another, on the
right with space to its left, or lower down with a gap above it. Widgets never overlap. A widget
can take a sixth, a quarter, a third, half, two thirds or more of the page's width, one twelfth at
a time.

Open **Settings → Widgets → Arrange widgets**, or long-press a widget on the widgets page. The
screen shows the page as it is:

- **Tap** a widget to choose it. A toolbar comes up at the bottom, and the widget gets handles on
  its right and bottom edges.
- **Long-press and drag** a widget to move it. Lines show the columns, and a dashed box shows where
  it will land. Dropped near another widget's edge, it lines up with it. Widgets in the way move
  down to make room. The space a widget leaves stays empty.
- **Drag a handle** to make the widget wider, narrower, taller or shorter: a twelfth of the page or
  8dp at a time, never below the size the widget needs.
- **Drop a widget on Remove**, at the top while you drag, to remove it. Kolahal asks first.
- **Close gaps**, in the top bar, moves every widget up as far as it goes.
- **+**, in the top bar, adds a widget.

For one of Kolahal's widgets or a script's widget, the toolbar has:

| Row | Buttons |
| --- | --- |
| Look | Plain, Tonal, Bold, Split, Shape, Poster, and for some widgets a look of their own: Now, Next or Stack ([below](#looks)) |
| Color | Accent, Link, Good, Warning, Bad, Muted, Text, the card's own first ([below](#looks)) |
| Shape (in the Shape look) | six shapes, the card's own first ([below](#looks)) |
| Size | 1×1, 2×1, 2×2, 4×1, 4×2, 4×4 |
| Days (Agenda only) | Auto, 1, 3, 7, 14 |

It also has **Options** (for a script with options) or **Edit shortcuts** (for Shortcuts),
**Remove** and **Done**.

For another app's widget, the toolbar has:

| Button | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Width − / + | narrower or wider, a twelfth of the page at a time |
| Height − / + | shorter or taller, 8dp at a time (never below the size the widget needs) |
| Square | as tall as it is wide, or as close as the widget allows |

Hold − or + to repeat. Kolahal gives each widget its real width and height, so it fits the space.

It also has **Widget settings** when the widget's app lets you change its setup ([below](#change-a-widgets-settings)),
**Remove** and **Done**.

With TalkBack, each widget on this screen has actions to move it up, down, left or right, to make it
wider, narrower, taller or shorter, to open its **Options**, **Edit shortcuts** or **Widget settings**
(when it has them), and to remove it.

### Change a widget's settings

Another app's widget can have a setup screen, for example to pick a city, a calendar or a note. To
change what you picked:

1. Long-press the widget, or open **Settings → Widgets → Arrange widgets** and tap it.
2. Tap **Widget settings** in the toolbar. The widget's setup screen opens.
3. Change what you want and save it there. Back leaves the widget as it was.

The widget keeps its place and size. **Widget settings** shows only for a widget whose app says its
setup can run again. For other widgets, remove the widget and add it again.

### Looks

Kolahal's own widgets and scripts' widgets share most looks, and a few widgets have a look of
their own. Each placed widget has its own look, so two weather widgets can look different. Choose
it in the toolbar's **Look** row.

| Look | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Plain | the card in the theme's surface color |
| Tonal | the card in its color, with lighter rows and chips |
| Bold | the main value large, in the card's color |
| Split | the card in separate tiles: one for the main value, one for each row or button |
| Shape | a 1×1 or 2×2 card is a shape (a cookie, a clover, a sun); in a wider card the main value sits in one. Buttons and switches each take a shape. Some shapes change with what they show: a countdown's sun loses a ray each day, a habit's week is a flower with a leaf for each day done, and the battery goes from round when full to spiky when low. A picture is cut to a shape |
| Poster | the value so large that the card's edge crops it, with the label small in a corner. Only a number, a countdown and a clock have it |
| Next | Todos: the next todo large with a check to tap, and a ring of the ticked ones. Reminders: the next one in a pill that empties as its time comes, and the later ones on a line |
| Stack | Notes: the newest note on a stack of sheets, and + for a new note |
| Now | what is on now and how far along it is, the free time until the next event, and that event. A 2×2 or larger card has the day, with the free time between its events. Only an agenda has it |

The **Look** row shows only the looks that change the card. Buttons, switches and Now playing
have no **Bold**, a picture has only **Plain**, **Tonal** and **Shape**, and a match's score
(`versus`) has no **Shape**. Todos has no **Shape** either: its rows have checkboxes where Shape
draws its shapes. It has **Next** instead. If a script shows another kind of card later, a look you
chose for it comes back when the kind has it.

The **Color** row under **Look** gives the card one of the theme's colors: accent, link, good,
warning, bad, muted or text. The first dot, a ring open in the middle, is the card's own color,
the one its script gives, so one tap gives it back. Tap another and the card takes it at once,
wherever it uses its own color: its ring, bar or chart, its big value in **Bold**, its shape in
**Shape**, its tinted card in **Tonal**. A script that changes its color with what it shows, such as
air quality turning bad, keeps doing so until you pick a color. Colors a script gives to a part,
such as a gauge's zones or a row, stay as they are. The Battery and network card keeps its warning
colors: bad when low, good while charging.

In the **Shape** look, a card that is one shape, or whose main value sits in one, has a **Shape**
row under **Color**: a 9-sided and a 12-sided cookie, a 4-leaf and an 8-leaf clover, a sun and a
burst. The first is the card's own shape, so one tap gives it back. Tap another and the card takes
it at once. A countdown, a habit's week, the battery and the sky have no **Shape** row, as their
shape shows what they count. Nor do buttons, switches, lists, an agenda or several bars, where each
part takes a shape of its own.

Some looks leave out small details, such as the weather card's high, low and rain in **Shape**.
TalkBack still reads them. Another app's widget draws itself, so it has no looks.

## Uninstalled apps and updates

When an app is updated, its widgets keep working. When an app is uninstalled, Kolahal removes its widgets
from the page.

## Backups

A [backup](/guides/backup/) records which widgets you placed, with their size, place, look, shape and color. But other apps'
widgets themselves can't move between installs. So after an import:

1. Open **Settings → Widgets → Arrange widgets**. The widgets that aren't on the page already are
   listed under **Restored from backup**.
2. Tap **Add again** on each widget to add it back. Run its setup if it has one.

Each widget keeps its size and position.

Kolahal's own widgets and your scripts' widgets need no **Add again**. They are back on the page after
the import.

- A backup holds where a card is, not what it showed. Each card fills again by itself.
- A script's further widgets come back too, each with the options you set for it.
- An imported script arrives switched off. Its card says "Script is off" until you turn the script on.
- Your shortcuts come back with the Shortcuts widget. The import result lists each shortcut that
  the file brought, so you can check what its button runs.

The same happens when Android restores Kolahal on a new phone from Android's own backup. A line in the log says
how many widgets you need to add again. Tap it to open that page.

## From the terminal

```sh
widgets        # open Settings → Widgets → Arrange widgets
widget add     # open the picker
widget ls      # list placed widgets (tap a line to open them); plain widget does the same
widget page    # turn home to the widgets page
```

`widgets` alone opens the screen; with a word after it, it works like `widget`: `widgets ls` is
`widget ls`.

See also the [command reference](/reference/commands/#widgets) and
[configuration keys](/reference/config/#widgets).

## Work profile

Work-profile widgets work like any other widget. They have a **work** badge in the picker.

While the work profile is paused, or the private space is locked, these widgets show a dim placeholder that says
"Work profile paused" or "Private space locked". They come back when you turn work apps on. A long-press on
the placeholder opens **Arrange widgets**, as on any widget. There it has no **Widget settings** until the
profile is on again.
