## Launching

To open an app, type part of its name and press Enter. The [basics](/getting-started/basics/#the-prompt)
page explains which app Enter picks. You can also use `open <app>` (alias `launch`). It does the same thing,
and it accepts profile flags:

```sh
open maps
open gmail -w
```

## Listing

```sh
apps                 # grid of chips (alias: ls)
apps -l              # one per line
apps hidden          # only hidden apps
apps -a              # include hidden apps
apps work            # one profile: work, personal, private
```

`apps` shows each app as a chip with its icon, in three columns. A name that is too long ends in "…".
A line above the apps says how many there are.

- When there are more apps than a few rows hold, it shows your pinned and most-used apps first,
  by name, then a chip such as **+26 more**. Tap it to show all the apps in place;
  **▴ Show less** shows the first ones again.
- `apps -l` lists every app, one per line.
- **Show app icons** (`suggestions.icons`) also turns these icons off.
- In the `tty` home style, the apps are in columns without icons, and **+26 more** is a line you tap.
- With a work profile or a private space, each profile has its own heading with its number of apps
  in place of that line: `── personal · 40 ──`, then `── work · 18 ──`. Work and private apps show
  the system's badge on their icon, or a letter before the name (W, S) without icons.

Tap a chip to open the app. Long-press a chip to see more actions:

- **Open**, **App info**, **Favorite**, **Hide** and **Uninstall**, in a row at the top
- the app's shortcuts, under **Shortcuts**, each with its own icon
- under **More**: the copy in another profile (**Work profile**, say) and **Copy package**

## Favorites

You can turn on **Favorites & most-used on empty prompt** (`suggestions.idle`, off by default). Then, when the prompt is empty, the suggestions show your pinned apps first, in the order you pinned them. Your most-used apps come after them.

```sh
pin maps                     # or long-press an app → Favorite
pins                         # grid of pinned apps
unpin maps
```

Pins are saved in the [backup file](/guides/backup/).

## Hide and rename

```sh
hide calculator              # gone from lists, search and suggestions
apps hidden                  # see what's hidden
unhide calculator
rename youtube = yt          # custom label
rename yt =                  # empty restores the original label
rename chrome -w = work web  # flags pick the profile
```

Hidden apps and custom labels are saved in the [backup file](/guides/backup/).

## App shortcuts

Kolahal supports app shortcuts. In other launchers, you see these when you long-press an app icon.
They only work when Kolahal is your default home app.

```sh
shortcuts maps               # list them; tap one to start it
open maps:home               # start the shortcut whose label best matches "home"
```

Shortcuts also appear in the action sheet of every app.

## Any screen of an app

An app has more screens than the one its icon opens. Some of them may be opened by other apps:
a settings page deep inside the app, or a screen with no button at all.

```sh
activity settings            # list them; tap one to open it
activity settings wifi       # open the screen that matches "wifi", or list them if several do
```

- Each line shows the screen's name, with its class name under it. When a screen has no name of
  its own, the line shows the class name.
- The name you type can be the class name (`WifiSettingsActivity`), the shown name (`Wi-Fi`) or
  the start of either. Case doesn't matter. When several screens match, Kolahal lists them.
- Long-press a line and choose **Copy command**. Use it in an alias: `alias wifi = activity Settings WifiSettingsActivity`.
- Only apps in the personal profile. Android doesn't let a launcher open these screens in a work profile.
- Some screens expect data from the app that opens them. They may close at once or show an error.
  That is the app's choice, not a fault in Kolahal.

## Aliases

```sh
alias m = open maps          # define
alias                        # list
alias m                      # show one
unalias m                    # remove
```

An alias replaces the first word of a command. It works like an abbreviation in the fish shell: type
its name at the start of the line, then a space, and the prompt shows what it stands for. With
`alias a = Authy`, typing `a` and a space leaves `Authy ` in the prompt. Press Enter, or add more
words first. Picking an alias from the suggestions expands it too.

- Only the first word expands. `echo a ` stays as it is.
- Enter also runs an alias you didn't expand: `a`, then Enter, opens Authy.
- Aliases can use other aliases, up to five levels deep, but they can't form a loop.
- You can't use a built-in command name as an alias.

## App info and uninstall

```sh
info whatsapp
uninstall some-game -w
```

### Install, update and uninstall log

When you install, update or uninstall an app, the log shows a line for it:

- The first line has the app name, a profile tag and a sign at the end: `+` for install, `↑` for update (a newer version),
  `−` for uninstall. Example: `Updated Maps [W] ↑`.
- The package name and version appear on dimmed lines below.

An update always shows as one line. If you uninstall an app and reinstall it within 20 seconds, that also shows as one line.
Because of this 20-second wait, an uninstall line appears about 20 seconds after the app is removed.
If you return to the home screen after those 20 seconds, it appears then.

If apps change while the launcher isn't running, Kolahal logs the changes the next time it starts.
`installs [<n>]` lists the recent history with dates.

## Screen time and unlocks

```sh
usage            # time per app today (alias: screentime)
usage 7          # last 7 days (1–30)
usage whatsapp   # one app: today, by hour, and the last 7 days
unlocks          # today's unlock count
unlocks 7        # one line per day for the last week (1–30)
unlocks month    # a column a day for 30 days, the trend, and a line per week
usage summary    # what Android keeps: days, weeks, months, years
usage month      # a row per month: its total and its time a day
usage full       # all of them: the summary, weeks, months and years
usage month youtube   # one app over the months
```

`usage` needs **Usage access** (`allow usage`). To count unlocks, Kolahal uses usage access if you granted it.
If not, it counts `ACTION_USER_PRESENT` broadcasts, but only while the launcher is running.

[How Kolahal counts screen time](/blog/how-usage-counts/) explains what `usage` counts, and why its numbers can differ from Digital Wellbeing's.

`usage <app>` shows one app: its time today, how often you opened it, its longest session and when
you last used it. Under that, a column for each hour today (or of the last day you used it, when
that was before today), then a line for each of the last 7 days with their average. It works for the apps of your personal profile: Android gives Kolahal no usage
of work apps or the private space.

Android keeps usage data for the last days only, about 10 on many phones. `usage 30` and `unlocks 30`
leave out the days before that, and their average is over the days that are there. Kolahal also
saves each day's unlock count, so `unlocks` goes back further as the weeks go by.

`unlocks month` draws a column for each day with a count, says how the last 7 days compare with the
7 before, and has a line for each week (Monday to Sunday) with its average. `unlocks week` draws
the columns of the last 7 days. The days before the first count are left out: at first there are
about 10 days, and more as Kolahal saves each day.

Every "a day" average counts the days before today only: today has only its hours so far, and
would pull the average down every morning. The comparison of two weeks counts finished days too.

### Daily limits

You can set a limit for a day's screen time and one for a day's unlocks, in **Settings → Header →
Daily limits** or with `config`:

```sh
config set usage.screen-time-limit 180   # 3 hours, in minutes
config set usage.unlock-limit 60
config set usage.unlock-limit 0          # no limit
```

From the limit on, today's figure is in the theme's warning color; from one and a half times the
limit, in its error color. `usage` and `unlocks` say it in a line under the figure too: `30m over
your limit of 3h`, `At your limit of 60`. `unlocks 7` colors each day's line by its own count. The
header's `unlocks` part, `top` and `fetch` color today's count, and a screen reader hears the
header's part say it. A limit changes nothing else: no notification, nothing blocked.

Without usage access, **Daily limits** shows "Needs usage access" and an **Allow usage access** button in place of the
two limits, and **Arrange the header** says the same on the Unlocks part. A limit you set stays, and `config` still
changes it.

### The long view

Besides its days, Android keeps coarser totals: about 4 weeks, 6 months and 2 years. `usage` reads
them with a word after it:

- `usage summary`: a line for each level, where it starts and the screen time a day in it.
  Tap a line to see that level.
- `usage week`, `usage month` and `usage year`: the time and unlocks a day, the total, and a table with
  a row for each week, month or year: its dates, its total and its time a day. Then the change of the
  last one that is over (▼ 13%) and the top apps.
- `usage year` also has the top apps of each year, and the apps you used <em>less</em> or
  that are <em>new</em> since the year before, each with a chip.
- `usage full`: all of these, each part after a `── weeks ──` line.
- With an app after `week`, `month` or `year`, such as `usage month youtube`: that app
  alone. Its time a day, its total and its share of all your screen time; a row for each week, month
  or year, with "0m" where it wasn't used; its change, and when you last used it. As for `usage <app>`,
  it works for the apps of your personal profile.

Scripts read the same numbers with `usage.summary` ([API](/scripting/api/#usagesummaryperiod-app)),
and the library's **screen-time-months** widget shows them as a bar a month.

The output keeps to numbers and short labels. A `≈` marks Android's own totals: about right, not
exact. Android starts each week, month and year where the one before ended, not on a Monday or the
1st, so each date is the real start of one. The current one counts in an average only once it has
run a week. Every app counts, the home screen, calls and Kolahal too.

A word is the word only on its own, or before an app's name: `usage week` shows the weeks, and
`usage weekly` the app Weekly. `summary` and `full` take no app, so `usage summary pro` is the app
Summary Pro. The suggestions under the prompt offer the words and the apps together.

When you uninstall or reinstall an app, Android deletes its history from some levels and not others:
from the days and the year at once, and from the weeks and months later or not at all. So a year
counts each app at least as much as the months inside it hold.

An app that Android doesn't name shows by its package name, such as `com.reddit.frontpage`. That is
an app you removed while Android still has time for it, a part of the phone with no name of its own,
or an app Android hides from Kolahal: since Android 11 an app sees only the apps it asks for, and
Kolahal asks for those with an icon or a widget. Kolahal can't tell which, so it doesn't say.


## Data and storage

```sh
data today       # mobile and Wi-Fi data today, per app
data month       # since the day your plan starts again, per app
storage          # free space, the largest apps, and apps not opened for 30 days
```

`data` alone opens Android's Internet panel, as before. These need **Usage access** too.

- `data month` counts from the day in `data.start-day` (1 by default). Set your monthly plan in GB
  with `config set data.plan 75`: then it shows how much of it you used, the pace for the month,
  and the days left. The bar turns red when the pace goes over the plan.
- Android gives Kolahal the data of each app in your personal profile. The rest, such as the work
  profile's apps, is in **Other**, so the columns add up to the totals.
- On Android 9, Android tells Kolahal only the Wi-Fi data per app.
- `storage` shows each app's size: the app and its data, cache included. The apps not opened for
  30 days or more are each a line: tap one to open it, or press and hold it for its info or to
  uninstall it. An app that Android has no use recorded for says so, with no day count. Apps of the
  phone's own that were never updated are left out: they can't be uninstalled.
